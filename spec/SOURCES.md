# Source and confidence register (agency handoff, 2026-11-24)

Published numerical summary facts are taken from the following pages. Full stage dry mass, usable propellant, aerodynamic tables and flight telemetry were not established consistently for each rocket, so no real vehicle is marked simulation-ready. The generic training models are invented educational test cases explicitly separate from the real catalog. They are not replicas. Stage profiles in GEOMETRY.md are artist's estimates except catalog dimensions; do not use them as engineering measurements. The library backlog is a future expansion list, not completed models. No observed flight data is included. All analytic test fixtures are calculated references, not completed software tests.

- SpaceX Falcon 9: https://new.spacex.com/vehicles/falcon-9 — summary height and published mass; thrust approximate conversion/rounding of reported 1.7 million lbf. Falcon 9 main diameter 3.7 m visual reference; NASA crew press kit https://www.nasa.gov/commercial-crew-program-press-kit/ .
- SpaceX Falcon Heavy: https://new.spacex.com/vehicles/falcon-heavy — height, three cores and 27 engines; no stage mass model.
- SpaceX Starship: https://new.spacex.com/vehicles/starship and company June 2026 prospectus https://content.spacex.com/cms-assets/FINAL_Documents%20and%20Updates/SpaceX%20-%20EU%20Prospectus%20%28Approved%20by%20Bafin%29%20-%20June%205%2C%202026.pdf — 124.4 m, 9 m configuration; other values intentionally omitted. Visual segmentation is approximate, not verified engineering data.
- NASA Saturn V overview https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-was-the-saturn-v/ — 111 m, about 2.8 million kg; historical data book https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/sp-4012v2.pdf Table 1-67 — stage lengths 42.1,24.9,17.9 m and diameters 10.1,10.1,6.6 m. Different mass definitions/configurations are not mixed. NASA history https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/SP-4206/ch1.htm describes engine counts.
- NASA SLS https://www.nasa.gov/reference/space-launch-system/ and https://www.nasa.gov/reference/sls-space-launch-system-core-stage/ and https://www.nasa.gov/reference/sls-space-launch-system-block-1b/ — Block 1 height 98.3 m, core diameter 8.4 m and maximum thrust about 39.1 MN; not a sea-level thrust assumption. Exact rounded 39,144 kN reported in NASA 2022 reference guide https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/sls_reference_guide_2022_print.pdf .
- Rocket Lab Electron https://rocketlabcorp.com/launch/electron/ — 18 m, 1.2 m, 13,000 kg, 300 kg published LEO payload, stage count and thrust/Isp figures. First-stage Isp reference condition not stated on summary, so do not treat as sea-level Isp.
- NASA Space Shuttle https://www.nasa.gov/reference/the-space-shuttle/ — configuration. NASA Crew Dragon press kit https://www.nasa.gov/commercial-crew-program-press-kit/ — catalog entry.
- JPL astrodynamic parameters https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html — DE440 GM values (km^3/s^2 converted to SI), Mars-system approximation. Body radii: https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html and https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/ ; rotation rates rounded standard mean assumptions, not ephemerides.
- NASA Glenn ideal rocket equation https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/ideal-rocket-equation/ and specific impulse https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/specific-impulse/ .
- U.S. Standard Atmosphere 1976: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19770009539 — lower-atmosphere layer approximation supplied, not full upper-atmosphere implementation. Mars exponential atmosphere and high-altitude truncations are explicitly selected educational approximations.
